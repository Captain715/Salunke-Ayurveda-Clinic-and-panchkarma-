Foundations

What is Ayurveda?

Ayurveda is a traditional Indian system of health knowledge. Its name is commonly translated as the knowledge of life.

Foundations

Vata, Pitta & Kapha

Classical Ayurveda describes three doshas as key functional principles. They are traditionally discussed in relation to individual constitution.

Daily living

Dinacharya

Dinacharya refers to daily routine in Ayurveda. It encourages attention to regular rhythms and everyday habits.

Seasonal living

Ritucharya

Ritucharya describes seasonal routines and the traditional idea of adapting habits as seasons change.

Food traditions

Ahara & mindful eating

Food is an important subject in Ayurvedic literature. Personal dietary choices should be discussed with an appropriately qualified professional.

Herbal knowledge

Herbs in Ayurveda

Ayurvedic texts discuss many plants. Herbs can have side effects and interactions; do not self-prescribe or replace medical care.

This library is educational, not medical advice. It does not diagnose, prevent or treat any condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for personal health decisions.

Botanical profile

Amla

Amla, also known as Indian gooseberry, is a fruit discussed in several Indian traditional knowledge systems. Its use and suitability depend on the individual.

Botanical profile

Tulsi

Tulsi is a culturally significant aromatic plant. Traditional uses are not a substitute for evidence-based care or professional advice.

Botanical profile

Turmeric

Turmeric is a familiar culinary spice and a subject of traditional use and modern research. Supplements may interact with medicines.

Botanical profile

Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha is discussed in Ayurveda, but it is not suitable for everyone and may have side effects or interactions.

Everyday living

Small rhythms. Considered living.

Explore traditional concepts around daily habits without treating them as rigid rules.

What is Dinacharya?

Dinacharya is a Sanskrit term commonly used for daily routine in Ayurveda. Traditional discussions may include waking, personal care, meals, activity and rest.

Make routines realistic.

A useful routine should fit a person's age, responsibilities, health and circumstances. There is no need to follow a strict schedule from a website.

A gentle reflection

  • Notice whether your sleep and wake times are reasonably consistent.
  • Make time for regular meals that suit your needs.
  • Include safe, age-appropriate movement.
  • Allow time to rest and seek help when something feels wrong.
Seasonal living

Understanding Ritucharya.

A traditional concept describing seasonal routines in Ayurveda.

Life changes with the seasons.

Ritucharya refers to seasonal regimen or seasonal routine. Classical Ayurvedic writing discusses how climate and seasonal transitions may influence everyday habits.

Practical choices should always consider local weather, personal comfort, medical needs and current public-health guidance.

Keep seasonal choices sensible

Stay hydrated, dress for the weather, follow food-safety guidance and seek medical advice for health concerns. Traditional concepts should not replace medical care.
